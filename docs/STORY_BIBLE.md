The campaign-scale narrative design (workstreams 10 and 12), built breadth-first
on top of the shipped Foundry Chapter. Sits under `docs/CREATIVE_BIBLE.md`:
where this document needs the bible amended, the request is in Appendix A and
nothing here pretends the amendment already happened. The shipped slice —
`data/encounters/e1`–`e5`, `docs/ENCOUNTER_NOTES.md`, the campaign roster — is
canon this document may enrich and may not contradict.

**Binding once accepted:** Layer 0 (the thesis and the ending's register),

Layer 2 beats marked **load-bearing**, and Layer 3a's plant→reveal pairs.
**Suggestive:** all other Layer 2 beats, Layer 3b/3c/3d except where marked,
and Layer 4 entirely (the sample scenes are register proof, not scenes to

**Canon rulings** (needed before anything else can be said):

1. The canonical playthrough of each shipped encounter is its **win path**:
   Wick broke for the stair and was taken; Nessa's gallery was reached; Aldric
   was downed on the top step.
2. **Downed is not dead** for named characters unless this document says so.
   Shipped canon already implies it: Aldric delivers `the-record-closes` after

## Layer 0 — The thesis

The campaign argues one sentence: **the same machine that renders the dead
into light renders the living into record, and the Compact will pay anything —
in anyone else's blood — to keep either ledger from being read.** Vein-glass
is the dead made fuel; the Inquiry is the living made paper; they are the same
industrial process run on different feedstock, and the Assay Sodality is the
institution that operates both. The tragedy's shape: Rowen Corvane, erased by
the record in Chapter 1, spends the campaign assembling the counter-record —
and at the end she does not publish it, because she has come to understand
exactly what the winners understand: the truth about vein-glass is not a bomb
you can throw only at the guilty. The Underveins starve first. So she does the
one thing the machine cannot refuse — she **files** the truth inside the
Assay's own archive, under the Assay's own unbreakable doctrine ("the Sodality
does not dispute findings; it files them"), sealed, attested, and unread.

**The ending's register**, decided now, keystone for everything below: FFT
ends with the truth recorded, the world unchanged, and the historian's frame
vindicated. Greyfall's equivalent: the truth is **filed, not published**; the
Compact goes on running on its dead under a politer vocabulary; and the frame
device closes by revealing that the game the player has been playing *is* the
archivist's merged document — the Inquiry record, Quill's duplicate
measurements, and Rowen's deposition and letters, annotated into one text
decades later. **Rowen's final state: erased but alive.** She is never
restored to the record under her own power; she is the counter-record's
author, and the campaign's last line is the archivist amending the record for
her: *no date of death*. The world does not change. The record does. That is
the whole victory, and the campaign must never apologize for its size.

## Layer 1 — The chapter spine

Five chapters. The three §12 seeds each get the chapter where they turn from
seed to engine: **Jory Slate's rise → Chapter 3. The vein-glass secret and
the Assay's doctrine → Chapter 4. The archivist frame → Chapter 5 and the
epilogue.** Chapter titles are dry and documentary, because every chapter is
named for the document that closes it.

### Chapter 1 — The Foundry Chapter *(shipped; summarized as-is)*

The Combine strikes the Corvane rail yards; Ensign Rowen Corvane, newly
commissioned under her brother Aldric, Master of the Watch, is sent to
suppress strike violence that turns out to be manufactured — provocateurs paid
through House channels to justify breaking the Combine and seizing an
Underveins seam claim. Across five battles she watches Perren Ash die and go
unlogged, finds unbroken Corvane requisition seals on the saboteurs'
explosives, watches an engineered overload at Refinery Three get filed as a
Combine act while Prelate-Assayer Quill records what he saw, and confronts
Aldric on the Charterhouse Steps, where he says "Yes." She wins the field and
loses everything else: the Inquiry records that she died in the refinery
accident, and she goes down by the tradesmen's lift. **What breaks:** the
strike, the Combine's position, and Rowen's name.

### Chapter 2 — The Finding

Weeks later. The Greyfall Inquiry sits in public session; the Combine is under
emergency proscription; House Corvane is recovering the narrative and Aldric
is cleared with honor. Rowen is nameless in the Works and the Underveins,
working rail gangs under the trade-name **Switch** — the House-trained body
crossing into Combine work (the Railrunner-off-Enforcer line in
`docs/CONTENT_NOTES.md` §1 was built for exactly this descent). She tries to
correct the record from outside: the docket from Tallow Row is routed to a
commissioner through a bought clerk, surfaces as "documents of doubtful
provenance," and is burned. Wick, bought out of Watch custody by the Ledger,
becomes her door into the Underveins; Sergeant Dray gets a warrant to hunt
"unidentified parties from the refinery" — a woman the record says is dead,
in the wording he himself wrote. Jory testifies truthfully, watches the panel
correct her recollection against the record, and takes the conciliation
clerkship: the inside game. Nessa Kiln is stripped and scapegoated; Vale
Tarn's licence is revoked in the same schedule; Quill files his duplicate and
is reassigned to the deep archive. In the seized seam claim, Gallery
Nine-Deep, old miners refuse a walled-up gallery — "dead in it" — and the
Assay quietly buys the worked-out ground. **What breaks:** the Final Finding
publishes (Combine act, full proscription, Rowen commended and dead), and
Rowen's belief that the record can be corrected from outside. She stops
appealing and starts archiving.

### Chapter 3 — The Conciliation

About two years on. Maud Corvane, the Seat, dies; Aldric takes the seat of
House Corvane amid Council maneuvering with House Bracken (refining,
Saltmere) and House Coll (deep mining, Coldelve). **Jory's rise turns
engine:** her Graft Standards and Brightblood Compensation Act is the
Compact's public argument, and she plays the houses that fund reform to weaken
each other. Maren, released, rebuilds the lodges and splits with her — the
bill compensates the dying and licenses the dying to continue. Rowen's crew
(the deserter company: Vale, Ivo, Della, Marek, Orin) runs recovery work —
documents, witnesses, people moved — and traces the provocateur pay-paper
across three cities: the frame-up of Chapter 1 was not a scheme, it is a
**product line**, brokered by the Ledger for whichever house needs a claim
made takeable, and its targets converge on fields the Assay has ruled
"impure." Nessa, doing unlicensed graft work in the Ledger's back rooms,
hands Rowen the question that drives Chapter 4: purity is a ruling, not a
measurement, and the Sedge — the fen where the Claim Wars ended — assayed
richest the same season it was interdicted. Dray, disgraced cornering the
company on the Assay's own metering floor, volunteers for the graft program.
**What breaks:** the Act passes with the Assay as its "neutral" enforcement
instrument — the Sodality's largest single accumulation of power — and the
Jory–Maren, Jory–Rowen, and Watch–Dray relationships all resolve into their
late-campaign shapes.

### Chapter 4 — The Render

The following year. Compensation costs bite; the houses need new seams; poor
House Marle challenges the Assay's interdict on the Sedge, which lies in
Wanefield's charter lands. **The vein-glass secret turns engine.** Embedded
with a Marle survey crew, Rowen's company opens a trench face in the fen and
finds bones in glass — vein-glass grown through a mass grave in ranks,
soldiers still in column: the Claim Wars' winter rout, sixty years rendered.
The knowledge was always held downward (the Underveins' pit rows, the miners'
sign over walled galleries) and never recorded upward. Quill surrenders the
charter's sealed annex: the founding survey, initialed by the framers —
including Rowen's grandfather — pricing the Sedge's yield in arithmetic dated
one month before the war's final campaign was ordered. Aldric reaches the
same truth by columns instead of trenches — the Assay has spent sixty years
buying worked-out galleries, holding dead House Cander's escheated claims "in
trust," and moving the interred by night — and dies within days in a tidy
mechanical accident the record does not examine. Maren dies of brightblood,
the Act's first posthumous award. Dray, grafted into the House's least human
instrument, is put down asking to be logged by name. **What breaks:** the
secret stops being a secret to Rowen and starts being a portfolio to the
Assay — Master of Standards Ansel Gault moves to convert the interdict into
open-ended "stewardship," and the Sodality learns that the dead ensign is
alive and holds the annex.

### Chapter 5 — The Deposition

Months later. **The archivist frame turns engine.** The seat war resolves
into the Restanding — a charter revision conference in Greyfall: a trades
seat for Jory (First Steward of the reformed Council), legitimacy for the
Ledger's money through House Coll's bought debts, royalties for Marle on a
Sedge worked under Assay stewardship. The truth is not defeated; it is
*managed* — the political apocalypse the vein-glass secret threatened is
priced, amortized, and signed. Jory's price is co-signing the stewardship
compact; her one unsigned act is opening the spire's door for Rowen. The
company argues out what the deposition is — publish and the amber goes dark
in every Underveins lamp by spring; Rowen chooses to deposit, not detonate —
and fights up the Assay spire to the Standards Hall, through the one
institution that files rather than fights, to Gault, who loses the field and
wins the outcome he prefers: the truth received, attested, sealed under
standard, and unread. Rowen goes down by the tradesmen's lift into no record
at all. **What breaks:** nothing in the world — that is the point — and
everything in the record. The epilogue, thirty years on, reveals the
annotator: **Archivist-Sodal Tam Ash**, Perren Ash's son from the yard,
keeper of the deep archive in Gallery Nine-Deep, third reader of Standing
Deposition 1, who receives the Tallow Row docket from an old woman who gives
no name, and amends the record: *Corvane, Rowen. No date of death.*

### The frame track — what the record claims vs. what happened

The gap **widens, then inverts**: early, the truth is what the player
witnesses and the record lies about it; by the end, the record is so total
that the truth survives only by *becoming* a record too. The inversion
completes when the counter-record is filed inside the record's own archive.

| Ch | The official record claims | What happened | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Strike violence suppressed; an ensign died with honor at Refinery Three | A frame-up executed through House channels; the ensign deserted | Opened |
| 2 | Final Finding: a Combine act; proscription justified; forgeries burned | The forgeries were genuine; the dead woman filed them | Widening — the record is confident |
| 3 | The Compact heals itself; reform proves the system works | Reform is the claims war by other means; the enforcement prize goes to the Assay | Widest — the record now writes the *future* |
| 4 | A season of accidents: a Seat dies, a fen is interdicted, graves are "robbed" | Management: a brother corrected, a truth converted to stewardship, the dead relocated | Inverting — Quill's duplicates and Rowen's archive now outweigh the record |
| 5 | The Restanding; "persons unknown repelled without loss to the record" | Nothing was taken; something was left — the truth, filed and sealed | Inverted — the truth is also a record now, and the archivist reads both |

## Layer 2 — Beats per chapter

Format: **[B]** battle-shaped (with its tactical premise in one clause — every
battle happens somewhere that works for a living), **[S]** scene-shaped.
**(LB)** load-bearing — the story breaks if it moves; **(FX)** flexible.
New named characters get one-line identities at first entry. Battle counts:
5 (shipped) + 4 + 4 + 4 + 3 = **20 campaign battles**, suggestive.

### Chapter 1 — The Foundry Chapter *(all beats shipped canon, binding as-is)*

1. **[B] The Marshaling Yard** (LB) — break a blockade on a working rail yard
   (switches, freight lift); a second maul comes out from *behind* the picket
   and goes over the wall as "an unidentified party leaving the scene."
2. **[B] Foundry Floor Nine** (LB) — suppress a "riot" on a floor someone
   kept running (presses, pour-ladle, catwalks); Perren Ash — *foundry hand,
   thirty years on the pour, a wife on Tallow Row and a son in the yard* —
   dies warning both sides, and the sergeant logs numbers, not names.
3. **[B] Tallow Row** (LB) — pursue the saboteur cell through live-gas
   tenements; unbroken Corvane requisition seals on the charges; Wick — *the
   cell's demolition man, Underveins-born* — is taken; the docket goes into
   Rowen's coat, out of procedure.
4. **[B] Refinery Three** (LB) — race an engineered overload through powered
   machinery and fail on schedule; Nessa Kiln — *licensed conduit, the
   overload's engineer* — runs it signed and metered; Quill — *the Assay's
   observer* — records what he saw: the two documents are not required to
   agree.
5. **[B] The Charterhouse Steps** (LB) — climb three terraces of the estate
   to Aldric, who says "Yes," explains the seam claim, and closes the record
   on her: she will not be in the record; she will be in the refinery.
6. **[S] The tradesmen's lift** (LB) — the desertion; shipped as e5's closing
   line and binding as the chapter's exit image.
7. **[S] Chapter document** (LB) — the Inquiry's First Finding publishes
   (text in Layer 3d).

### Chapter 2 — The Finding

1. **[S] The descent** (LB) — Rowen buys a trade-name and Wick, out on the
   Ledger's coin, vouches her onto a rail gang. Enter **Tally** — *the
   Ledger's bookkeep; prices everything, including silence* — who marks her:
   "You grip like a Watch officer. That costs extra to ignore."
2. **[B] The Coalyard Weighbridge** (LB) — a Watch sweep for unlicensed taps
   hits the gang mid-shift; premise: fight on a working weighbridge — moving
   hopper cars, a scale platform that tips under load. Establishes **Dray** —
   *the sergeant of e1–e5, now warranted* — hunting "unidentified parties
   from the Refinery Three incident": his own wording, come back to employ
   him. Rowen must not be taken or identified.
3. **[S] Jory's testimony** (LB) — Jory tells the Inquiry the truth about
   Floor Nine; the panel thanks her and corrects her recollection against the
   record. She takes the conciliation clerkship and leaves the company:
   "You keep the docket. I'm going to go be a paragraph in their document,
   and then I'm going to write the next one."
4. **[B] The Mercy Hall** (FX — placement; LB — Maren's choice) — the
   Combine's benefit hall, requisitioned as a proscription court; the company
   moves the lodges' records out ahead of seizure; premise: a fight over
   filing cabinets in a hall built for weddings and wakes — carrying crates
   is the objective. Maren declines to run: "The steward stays where the
   members can find her." Taken into detention.
5. **[S] The scapegoat** (LB) — the Assay strips Nessa's licence before the
   Inquiry; the same schedule voids the deserters' licences, Vale Tarn's
   among them — *young Assay-licensed conduit of Rowen's company, now legally
   a sparker*. Quill files his duplicate of Refinery Three and is reassigned
   to the deep archive. Plants: the Assay punishes conduct by adjusting
   paper, never by touching the number it measured.
6. **[B] Gallery Nine-Deep** (LB — the plant) — the seized seam claim, now
   Corvane-worked; the gang lays track under a Watch overseer who forces the
   far gallery the old miners refuse ("dead in it"); premise: evacuation
   under failing timber, live gas, and a raw seam — the first fight lit
   pale green instead of amber. Rowen sees vein-glass glowing through a
   walled-up gallery of the old pit rows, and sees the miners make a sign
   over it. Shown, never explained.
7. **[S] The appeal** (LB) — Tally brokers the docket to the Third
   Commissioner through a bought clerk (**Hessle** — *a records clerk who
   keeps copies, because clerks always keep copies*); it is examined,
   declared forged, and burned; Hessle is dismissed. The original never left
   her coat. This is the beat where appealing ends and archiving begins.
8. **[B] The Records Annex** (FX — placement; LB — function) — the Inquiry's
   clerks' floors on the lower Rise by night; the company steals the exhibit
   *list* — not the exhibits — to learn what the record contains; premise: a
   paper factory (pneumatic message lines, dumbwaiters, lamp rounds) where
   the hazard is being seen, and the mandate is low-kill.
9. **[S] The Finding published** (LB) — a crier reads it in the yard while
   the gang eats: the Combine proscribed; Ensign Rowen Corvane commended and
   dead, with a date. Vale: "They gave you a medal." Rowen: "They gave it to
   her. She's theirs." Chapter document (Layer 3d).

*Cast positions:* Jory inside; Maren detained; Wick and Tally established;
Nessa stripped; Quill archived; Dray warranted; Aldric cleared and absent
(his absence is the chapter's statement about him); the company outlawed
together — Ivo keeps the gang's tools, Della runs the rails, Marek knows the
Row, Orin's grafts ache in the cold (first line of his arc).

### Chapter 3 — The Conciliation

1. **[S] Maud's funeral** (LB) — enter and exit **Maud Corvane** — *the Seat
   of Corvane, the mother* — as a state occasion Rowen watches from the
   crowd; a letter, held by her law-house for "the holder of the Tallow Row
   docket," reaches Rowen through Tally: *"What you carry, carry further than
   your brother can reach."* The mother knew; the House's succession opens.
2. **[B] The Saltmere Bonding Sheds** (LB — the reveal beat for the product
   line) — following provocateur pay-paper into a Bracken bonded warehouse;
   premise: customs cranes, bonded stacks, and tide-gates that flood the
   lower floor on schedule. The paper shows one Ledger broker paying
   provocateur cells in three cities under three houses' names: the frame-up
   is a product line. Enter **House Bracken** — *refining and compounds,
   Saltmere; funds reform to weaken Corvane* — as terrain, not yet as faces.
3. **[S] The lodge argument** (LB) — Maren, released, at the re-founded
   lodge; Jory comes to sell the Act. Maren: "It compensates the dying and
   licenses the dying to continue." Jory: "It is what passes." The split,
   stated once, cleanly, and never restaged.
4. **[B] The Coldelve Winding House** (LB) — extracting **Assayer Lunn** —
   *a Survey man who wants out, carrying the impurity pattern* — from House
   Coll's deep headframe; premise: the winding engine — cages, drums, a
   vertical map where the fight rides the moving cage. Lunn's data: every
   field the Survey reads richest, the Assay rules impure. He hands it over
   and vanishes rather than testify.
5. **[S] Nessa in the graft-market** (LB — reveal 1's told-plant) — stripped,
   maintaining unlicensed grafts in the Ledger's back rooms; she gives Rowen
   the question, not the answer: "Purity is a ruling, not a measurement. I
   metered a Sedge tap myself — cleanest amber I ever drew. The impurity is
   in what it's drawn *from*. And they know what it's drawn from."
6. **[B] The Wanefield Grange** (LB) — a manufactured incident aimed at
   Jory's traveling commission — the product line turned on reform itself,
   commissioner unattributed and *left* unattributed; premise: a grange
   market on hearing day — a tapped grain-drier, packed pens, a crowd, and a
   protect-the-scene mandate. The company prevents it unseen; Jory glimpses
   Rowen alive. Enter **House Marle** — *land and grain, Wanefield; the
   poorest founding house, sitting on the interdicted fen* — via its Seat,
   **Edda Marle** — *stubborn, unbought, out of her depth*.
7. **[S] The night platform** (LB) — Rowen and Jory, once, alone (the
   intimate register scene; sample in Layer 4). Jory offers a clean name;
   Rowen declines a third document; Jory offers one door, once. Both offers
   stand until Chapter 5.
8. **[B] The Standards Floor** (LB — Dray's turn) — a licence-amnesty trap on
   the Assay's public metering floor, baited with Vale's revoked licence;
   premise: gauge pits, calibration flames, and a floor that is legally
   sacrosanct — the Watch may not damage it and the company may; asymmetric
   constraint as the tactical thesis. The company escapes; Dray is disgraced
   by the damage and volunteers for the graft program.
9. **[S] The Act passes** (LB) — enforcement entrusted to the Assay. Enter
   **Ansel Gault** — *Master of Standards, head of the Sodality; modest,
   exact, and the campaign's patient antagonist* — with one public sentence:
   "The Sodality seeks no seat. Standards do not sit. They stand under
   everything."
10. **[S] The statistic** (FX — placement; LB — content) — Maren's
    brightblood diagnosis is read into the commission record as a projected
    compensable case; the record has begun writing the future. Chapter
    document (Layer 3d).

*Cast positions:* Aldric takes the Corvane seat off-stage (his chapter is
Chapter 4); Wick brokers doors; Tally hedges; Orin's grafts accumulate — his
Resolve is visibly eroding (a beat inside 3.8: he freezes on the sacrosanct
floor, the first time the company sees it); Della and Ivo carry beats 2 and 4
respectively; Marek names the Row's version of everything.

### Chapter 4 — The Render

1. **[B] The Sedge Cut** (LB — the reveal) — embedded with Edda Marle's
   survey crew cutting a test trench against the interdict; the Assay fields
   its own instrument for the first time — **the wardens** — *enforcement
   inspectors with kit; the standards bureau armed*; premise: fen trenchworks
   — duckboards, pumps, rising water, raw seam everywhere (ambient flux:
   high-Attunement units empowered and endangered, Layer 3b). Mid-battle the
   trench face opens: **bones in glass — vein-glass grown through a mass
   grave in ranks, soldiers still in column.** Shown, not told; nobody says
   the sentence aloud in this beat.
2. **[S] The arithmetic** (LB — the thesis scene) — the company around a
   pump-house stove, seam maps laid over battle maps. Marek supplies the
   Underveins version: "The pit rows. The old city walled them up and the
   glass came. Everyone's grandmother knew. Nobody said it *upward*." The
   truth has two registers too — held downward, never recorded upward.
3. **[S] The trust lands** (LB) — Aldric and Gault (the political register
   scene; sample in Layer 4). Aldric arrives with columns: the Assay's
   sixty years of buying worked-out galleries, dead House Cander's claims
   held "in trust," the interdict on the richest field in the Compact — and
   leaves knowing, having been told nothing and everything: "Your grandfather
   signed the annex. It is the finest hand on the page."
4. **[B] The Interment Yards** (LB — the method shown) — the Clean Grounds
   Office's terraced cemetery over old galleries, worked at night: the
   Assay *moves* the dead, consolidating interments over ground it controls —
   not farming death, curating deposition; the horror stays logistical;
   premise: a cemetery that is also a working yard — bier hoists, lime cars,
   open gallery mouths, and a night-work crew that must not be harmed.
5. **[S] The brother** (LB) — Aldric summons "the holder of the docket"
   through Maud's channel and meets his dead sister. No apology; a handover:
   the House's Sedge portfolio ledger. "It is not a claim, it is a confession
   with a yield curve." Then: "Where a thing is signed, Rowen—" / "—is not
   where it happens. I remember." He dies within days: a winding-house
   accident, tidy, unexamined, and deliberately never resolved on the page
   (Layer 3a, entry 7).
6. **[B] The Watch Graftworks** (LB — motif closure) — Dray, grafted heavy,
   corners the company in the shop where the House turns men into
   instruments; premise: charge baths, restraint frames, a room made of the
   Augmented job's price, where Grounding is the tactical answer. Downed, he
   asks for the thing he refused Perren Ash: "There's a number on my warrant.
   I had a name once. Log it." Rowen logs it. — Inside or adjacent: **Orin's
   rejection crisis** (FX with a constraint: he is Grounded and survives
   graft-retired, *or* he dies; no third outcome, and the choice belongs to a
   later design pass).
7. **[S] The lodge book** (LB — thematically; FX — placement) — Maren dies;
   the compensation letter arrives after; the lodge reads her name into its
   book. The Combine has always kept the counter-record: the unions log
   names. Della takes up the book.
8. **[S] The annex** (LB) — Quill, in the deep archive, surrenders the
   charter's sealed annex: the founding survey, sixty years old, the framers'
   initials on it. Its marginal arithmetic prices the Sedge's yield — dated
   one month before the final winter campaign of the Claim Wars was ordered.
   The annotator will note the dates and decline to conclude. Quill's cost:
   he stays, because the archive must have a keeper who knows what it keeps.
9. **[B] The Pump House** (LB) — the wardens, with hired Ledger muscle
   (everyone uses the Ledger), come for Marle's trench records and the
   company's copies; premise: the fen pump house at night — fire against
   rising water, high-charge Assay kit against a map with one dry floor.
   The company saves what it carries and loses the rest; Wick walks them out
   through gas he has known longer than the Assay has known him.
10. **[S] The file** (LB) — Gault reads the Sodality's own counter-record: of
    *her*. The Assay knows Rowen is alive and does not correct the Inquiry —
    "She is dead in the record. That is a fact about the record." Chapter
    document (Layer 3d).

*Cast positions:* Jory spends the chapter paying the Act's costs in public

### 3b. Mechanics × story — where the systems are the narrative

The bible demands the stat pair and the body-horror be story material, not
flavor. The touchpoints, each anchored to a beat:

- **Rule §5.4 is the economy.** "The dead stay dead" is why permadeath is
  real *and* why vein-glass exists: a world with resurrection has no corpse
  to render. The campaign's horror is the mechanic's world-scale corollary,
  and 4.2 is where the player is allowed to notice.
- **Rowen's job line is her class descent.** Railrunner-off-Enforcer was
  built for this (`CONTENT_NOTES` §1): Chapter 2 makes it literal — the
  House-trained body earning Combine bread on the rails. Suggested: the
  campaign's Chapter 2 progression nudge is her Railrunner unlock, not a
  cutscene.
- **Attunement is legible, and sometimes the point is that it doesn't
  change.** Vale's licence revocation (2.5) alters no number on her sheet —
  the Assay measured her once and the measurement stands; only the paper
  moved. The story should linger on the non-event: the licence never
  conferred anything. Conversely, story-driven permanent shifts (bible §7
  allows them) are reserved for keystone beats — candidates, suggestive:
  Perren's death and the Steps for Rowen's Resolve; the trench face (4.1)
  for small Attunement shifts across everyone present; the brother scene
  (4.5) for Rowen.
- **The Augmented arc is Dray and Orin in mirror.** Grafts trade Resolve for
  Attunement (bible §7). Dray — the campaign's highest-Resolve voice, the
  procedure made flesh — volunteers to become the instrument, and his boss
  sheet in 4.6 should *be* his tragedy: Resolve collapsed, Attunement
  towering, Grounding the answer. Orin runs the same arithmetic inside the
  party, and 3.8/4.6 want an ally-targetable Ground (design ask, below).
- **Ambient-flux maps.** The Sedge battles (4.1, 4.9) sit on raw seam:
  high-Attunement units stronger and more exposed at once — the §7 sentence
  ("power and vulnerability in the same number") given terrain. Encounter
  design ask, suggestive.
- **Faction charge economies as voice.** The Watch does not run on flux
  (`CONTENT_NOTES` §3); the Assay wardens are its inverse — high-charge kits
  on powered maps. Chapter 4–5 enemy design should read as: the Watch hits
  you with procedure, the Assay with infrastructure.
- **The player's dead.** Permadeath plus the lodge book (4.7): fallen roster
  units should be logged by name in a campaign-persistent record the player
  can read — the Combine's counter-record given to the player as UI.
  Suggestive, cheap, and exactly on-theme.
- **Design asks surfaced to mechanics/content agents** (not story
  requirements — the beats above survive without them): ally-targetable
  Ground; ambient-flux tile property; a campaign flag for Orin's 4.6 fork;
  scripted permanent disposition shifts (engine already stores disposition;
  needs a campaign-layer hook).

## Layer 4 — Sample scenes *(register proof only; suggestive, not canon;

### Scene 1 — political. "The Trust Lands" (Chapter 4, beat 3)

*The Master of Standards' office, high in the spire. Bare. One wall gauge
shows the city's draw, ticking. GAULT stands. He has not offered a chair and
ALDRIC has not looked for one. A leather folder of column-work under Aldric's
arm.*

**ALDRIC:** Your office bought Gallery Nine-Deep from my house at four times
its assay. My own surveyor calls it dead rock.

**GAULT:** The Sodality holds ground in trust in all four cities. Exhausted
galleries are dry, stable, and deep. We archive in them.

**ALDRIC:** You archive.

**GAULT:** We are a body of record, Seat Corvane. Records accumulate.

**ALDRIC:** Then the Cander lands. Sixty years in your trust — never worked,
never sold, never surveyed twice. And the Wanefield fen, interdicted for
impurity the same season your Survey read it richer than anything in the
charter. *(he sets the folder down, open)* I have had these columns checked
three times. Every purchase your office has made in sixty years shares one
property, and it is not geology.

**GAULT:** *(he does not look at the folder)* What does your house want?
Precision costs less when the question is exact.

**ALDRIC:** I want to know what I have been selling you.

**GAULT:** Ground.

**ALDRIC:** Ground with dead in it. The trust lands are graves. The fen is a
grave. My gallery — *(he stops)*

**GAULT:** Your gallery is archived. *(a pause with nothing in it)* The
Sodality does not discuss doctrine outside the Table of Measures. But I will
offer you a standard to take home. A tolerance is not a lie, Seat Corvane. It
is an agreement about how exact a thing must be before the work can proceed.
The charter is a tolerance. The Compact proceeds.

**ALDRIC:** And what is out of tolerance?

**GAULT:** Corrected. *(he opens the door himself)* The Sodality thanks House
Corvane for its custom. Your grandfather signed the annex, incidentally. It
is the finest hand on the page.

*Aldric leaves with everything, having been told nothing. The gauge ticks.*

### Scene 2 — intimate. "The Night Platform" (Chapter 3, beat 7)

*Wanefield grange station, past midnight. The commission's special train
takes on water. JORY SLATE at the dark end of the platform, commission grey,
a folder she isn't reading. ROWEN in crew canvas walks the train, checking
axle-boxes that do not need checking.*

**JORY:** The crew chief says his tally-runner has walked this train twice
tonight. He thinks she wants his job.

**ROWEN:** Tell him his third axle-box is running hot, and his tally-runner
wants nothing he can lose.

**JORY:** *(a silence)* They told me you died at Refinery Three. I read it.
It had a schedule number.

**ROWEN:** Schedule D. I've read it too. I'm not in it. *She* is.

**JORY:** So that's the arrangement. A dead woman, and — what's the name on
your book?

**ROWEN:** Switch.

**JORY:** Switch. *(she doesn't laugh; something near it crosses and goes)*
I sign forty documents a week now. I could sign one more. Not your name — a
name. Clean paper. A licence. A life that happens above ground.

**ROWEN:** A third document. The record says one thing. I know another. Now
paper that says a third — signed by you. How many documents before one of
them is a lie you told me?

**JORY:** It's how everything gets done, Rowen. You've seen it from
underneath now.

**ROWEN:** I've seen what it costs from underneath. Yesterday at the grange —
that was aimed at you. Same shop that aimed one at me, three years ago. You
are useful at the front of a crowd. One day you'll be more useful at the
front of a finding.

**JORY:** I know what I'm standing on. I've known since Floor Nine. You put
your foot where the floor holds, and you pour anyway. That's the trade.

**ROWEN:** Perren Ash held thirty years.

**JORY:** *(long pause)* That isn't fair.

**ROWEN:** No. Nothing about him was.

*The train answers the water crane. Somewhere forward, a lamp swings.*

**JORY:** If you ever need a door opened — one door, once — send the book.
Just the book. I'll know the hand.

**ROWEN:** Then I'll spend it carefully.

**JORY:** Go check something. My clerk logs anyone who speaks to me longer
than a minute.

**ROWEN:** *(stepping back into the dark)* Log me as nobody.

*Jory watches the dark where she went. Then she opens the folder and reads.*

### Scene 3 — late-campaign frame. "The Fourth Reader" (epilogue, beat 5.9)

*The deep archive, Gallery Nine-Deep. Shelving runs into dark that used to be
a seam. TAM ASH, Archivist-Sodal, at a work table under one amber lamp, the
annotated Inquiry record open at its last page. A PORTER with a small parcel,
canvas-wrapped.*

**PORTER:** Left at the spire gate, sir. No name on it. Gatekeeper says an
old woman in a canvas coat put it in his hands and went down by the tram
before he could sign for it.

**TAM:** Everyone signs for everything here. It's the one thing we're for.

*He unwraps it. A docket, decades old, Corvane Freight stencil, requisition
numbers. Folded inside, a slip of paper in a steady hand. He reads it once to
himself. Then, because the archive keeps what is said aloud in it better than
anywhere in the Compact keeps anything:*

**TAM:** "Perren Ash. Thirty years on the pour. Logged by name, this ninth of
Coaling, by R.C., ensign, House Watch. The sergeant would not."

*A long silence. The lamp. The dark that used to be a seam.*

**PORTER:** Sir?

**TAM:** My father. *(he sets the slip flat on the table and smooths it once,
the way you smooth a thing you will never move again)* He had a wife on
Tallow Row and a son in the yard. The son got clean work in the end.
Standards work. *(he takes up the pen)* You can go, Corse. Log the parcel as
received.

**PORTER:** Received from whom, sir?

**TAM:** *(writing)* From the author.

*What he writes, the last annotation in the record:*

> *Standing Deposition 1 has a fourth reader now, and the seal held thirty
> years, which is longer than most walls. The record is amended as follows.
> CORVANE, ROWEN. Ensign. Deserter. Depositor. No date of death. —Archivist-
> Sodal T. Ash, the Archive, Gallery Nine-Deep.*
